COPD CKS Summary (250 words)
Definition & Diagnosis

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a progressive airflow limitation, not fully reversible, typically caused by smoking.

Diagnosis confirmed by post-bronchodilator spirometry: FEV₁/FVC < 0.7.

Consider in patients >35 with risk factors (smoking, occupational exposure) and symptoms (dyspnoea, cough, sputum, wheeze).

Exclude asthma: consider variability, family history, eosinophilia.

Assessment & Monitoring

Assess severity via spirometry (FEV₁ % predicted), MRC dyspnoea score, exacerbation history, and comorbidities.

Monitor symptoms, exacerbation frequency, inhaler technique, smoking status, and oxygen saturation.

Consider pulse oximetry and spirometry annually or as clinically indicated.

Management

Smoking cessation is the most effective intervention.

Offer annual influenza and one-off pneumococcal vaccinations.

Use inhaled bronchodilators (SABA or SAMA) initially.

Escalate to LABA + LAMA or LABA + ICS if indicated by symptoms/exacerbations.

Triple therapy (LABA + LAMA + ICS) for frequent exacerbations with asthmatic features or raised eosinophils.

Pulmonary rehabilitation for patients with MRC dyspnoea score ≥3.

Antibiotics if sputum is purulent and signs of infection.

Exacerbations

Defined as acute worsening of symptoms.

Treat with increased bronchodilators, short course of oral steroids, and antibiotics if indicated.

Offer self-management plan and rescue pack for frequent exacerbators.

Referral

Consider referral to specialist if uncertain diagnosis, rapid decline, severe disease, or long-term oxygen therapy needed.

Summary
Definition & Diagnosis
COPD = persistent, progressive airflow obstruction (not fully reversible), usually due to smoking.

Suspect in age ≥35, chronic cough, dyspnoea, sputum, wheeze, and risk factors.

Confirm with spirometry: post-bronchodilator FEV₁/FVC <0.7.

Differentiate from asthma via history, symptom variability, and eosinophil count.

Assessment & Monitoring
Assess FEV₁ % predicted, MRC dyspnoea score, exacerbation frequency, BMI, oxygen saturation.

Regularly monitor symptoms, exacerbations, inhaler technique, smoking status.

Management
Smoking cessation is the most important intervention.

Offer annual flu and one-off pneumococcal vaccines.

Stepwise bronchodilator use:

Start with SABA/SAMA → escalate to LABA + LAMA or LABA + ICS.

Triple therapy if symptoms persist or frequent exacerbations.

Pulmonary rehab if MRC dyspnoea ≥3.

Provide self-management plan and rescue pack if needed.

Exacerbations
Acute symptom worsening.

Manage with increased inhalers, steroids (e.g. prednisolone), and antibiotics if sputum is purulent.

Referral
Refer for diagnostic uncertainty, rapid decline, severe COPD, or need for long-term oxygen.
